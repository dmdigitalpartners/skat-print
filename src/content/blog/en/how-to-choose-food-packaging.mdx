Choosing packaging for a food product involves more decisions than most buyers expect. The packaging brief that arrives on a manufacturer's desk typically lists box dimensions and a print file. What it often doesn't account for is substrate grade, food-contact compliance, shelf-readiness, transit performance, and minimum order economics — all of which affect whether the packaging actually works for its intended purpose.

This guide covers the key variables to consider when specifying food packaging, with a focus on corrugated and cardboard formats for B2B food brands selling through retail and distribution channels.

## Material Selection: Corrugated vs. Cardboard

The first decision is structural: corrugated or folding carton?

**Corrugated board** (with a fluted inner layer) provides compression strength, stacking resistance, and protection through transit. It's the right choice for transport boxes, shelf-ready packaging, and any format that needs to survive a distribution chain.

**Folding cartons** (solid cardboard) are used for primary consumer-facing packaging — cereal boxes, tea packaging, individual product boxes. They're printed and finished for shelf appeal, not structural durability.

Most food supply chains use both: a printed folding carton for the product itself, and a corrugated outer case for transit and warehouse handling. When specifying packaging, it's worth being clear which you need — or whether you need both.

## EU Food-Contact Compliance

Any packaging that comes into direct contact with food — or may come into indirect contact through migration — must comply with EU Regulation 1935/2004 on materials and articles intended to contact food.

For corrugated and cardboard packaging, the key compliance considerations are:

- **Mineral oil aromatic hydrocarbons (MOAH)** — a known concern for recycled paperboard; look for virgin fibre or certified low-MOAH substrates for primary food contact
- **Inks and coatings** — food-safe ink systems must be used for any packaging in direct food contact
- **Documentation** — your packaging supplier should be able to provide a Declaration of Compliance for food-contact materials

If your packaging is indirect contact only (sealed inner bag inside a corrugated outer case, for example), the compliance requirements are less stringent — but you should still verify with your supplier.

## FSC Certification: When It Matters

FSC (Forest Stewardship Council) Chain of Custody certification is increasingly required by:

- Retailers with sustainability sourcing policies (Lidl, Rewe, Carrefour and most major EU chains have active FSC requirements)
- Organic and natural food brands with eco-label commitments
- Export customers in markets where FSC is an expected baseline

If your retail customer requires FSC-certified packaging, your manufacturer must hold FSC Chain of Custody certification — not just source from FSC-certified forests. Ask for the certificate number and verify it directly on the FSC database.

## Shelf-Ready Packaging (SRP)

Shelf-ready packaging — also called retail-ready packaging — is a corrugated outer case that opens cleanly to display products directly on the shelf without repacking.

Key design requirements for SRP:

- **Easy-open perforations** that allow store staff to open the case without tools
- **Clean tear line** with no cardboard tearing into the display area
- **Planogram compatibility** — dimensions that match your retailer's shelf depth and facing width
- **Print on the open face** so the display is branded when opened

Most major European retailers have SRP specifications. Get these from your buyer before finalising the brief with your packaging manufacturer.

## MOQ Planning for Food Brands

Minimum order quantities for food packaging depend on format complexity, substrate, and print method. As a general guide:

| Format | Typical MOQ |
|--------|-------------|
| Standard corrugated case (plain or 1-colour) | 200–500 units |
| Corrugated case with full-colour offset print | 500–1,000 units |
| Shelf-ready packaging with die-cut perforations | 1,000–2,000 units |
| Folding carton with 4-colour print | 500–2,000 units |

If your volumes sit below typical MOQs, consider whether a short-run digital print solution makes sense for market testing before committing to offset production runs.

## Working with a Packaging Manufacturer

When issuing a packaging brief, the information your manufacturer needs to give you an accurate quote:

1. **Dimensions** — internal dimensions (length × width × height) with tolerances
2. **Product weight** — affects board grade and stacking specification
3. **Print artwork** — or a brief for pre-press artwork development
4. **Finish requirements** — plain kraft, 1-colour print, full-colour offset, lamination
5. **Quantity** — and whether you need flexibility around the MOQ
6. **Compliance requirements** — food-contact, FSC, retailer SRP specs
7. **Lead time** — standard or urgent

The more complete the brief, the fewer revision cycles before production approval.
